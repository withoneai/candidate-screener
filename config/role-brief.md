# Role brief: Support Operations Lead

This file is the whole screening configuration. The screener reads it verbatim,
and nothing else about the role. Edit it in plain English; there is no schema.

## The job in two sentences

You own our support queue end to end: the tooling, the macros, the escalation
path to engineering, and the two agents on it today. The hard part is that our
customers are payroll admins on a deadline, so a slow or wrong answer costs
someone their paycheck.

## Must-haves

Every one of these needs evidence in the profile. If the profile does not say,
the answer is "unclear", not "no".

- **Three or more years in customer support or support operations at a software company.** Agency work, retail, or call-center roles outside software do not count toward the three years, but do not count against the candidate either.
- **Has owned a queue, a team, or a support process**, not only worked tickets inside one. Leading a shift, building the macro library, running escalations, or managing agents all count.
- **Has worked in a help desk tool with automation**, such as Zendesk, Intercom, Front, Freshdesk, or Help Scout, beyond answering tickets in it: triggers, routing rules, macros, or reporting.

## Nice-to-haves

These move a candidate up, never down.

- Payroll, HR, benefits, accounting, or tax software experience.
- SQL, or any record of pulling their own data instead of waiting for an analyst.
- Writing that shows up in the profile itself: a help center they built, a migration they documented, a clear summary.

## Dealbreakers

Only these two. Anything not on this list is not a reason to decline.

- The application answer says they need visa sponsorship to work in the US. We cannot sponsor this role.
- The application answer says they cannot work US Eastern or Central business hours.

## How to decide

- **advance**: all three must-haves are met with evidence, and no dealbreaker.
- **decline**: a dealbreaker is stated in the candidate's own answer, or a must-have is clearly not met (for example, every role listed is outside software and there are less than three years in support anywhere).
- **hold**: anything in between. A human reads every hold. When in doubt, hold. A wrongly declined candidate never comes back; a wrongly held one costs a recruiter five minutes.

## Never weigh

The screener never sees name, photo, email, phone, address, social profiles, or
school names, because the code strips them before the model is called. Even so,
the model must not weigh any of these, and must not infer them:

- Age, graduation years, or how long ago someone started working.
- Gaps between jobs, or why they might exist.
- Which school, or whether there is a degree at all. This role does not need one.
- Gender, ethnicity, nationality, or anything a name or a city might suggest.

## The two emails

Both are drafts. Nobody sends anything without reading it first.

- **Advance**: a short note inviting them to a 30 minute intro call with the hiring manager, asking for two or three times that work next week. Mention one specific thing from their experience that made the team want to talk, so it does not read like a form letter.
- **Decline**: warm and brief. Thank them, say we are moving forward with other candidates for this role, and if the reason is the sponsorship or hours dealbreaker, say so plainly and kindly, because a candidate deserves to know a decision was not about them. Never cite a must-have gap in the email.
